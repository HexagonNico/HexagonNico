## I do not use AI for coding

I refuse to use generative AI to complete any task on my behalf, regardless of whether it is for coding, creating images, or "generating ideas" (whatever that means).
I don't want my work to be used as training material for AI models.
I don't appreciate seeing generative AI used in the games I play, the software I use, or the content I consume.

### I don't need AI

I don't need AI for coding.
I learned to code for fun when I was in my teen years.
I have been programming since 2016 and have experience using several different languages and tools.
I already know how to code and don't need an AI to do it for me.

I don't need AI for generating images.
Over the years, I have worked with and commissioned several different artists and it has always been a more than pleasant experience.
I was able to connect with several different people by commissioning art.
The result has always surpassed my expectations.

I don't need an AI to generate ideas for me because I already have ideas of my own.
There are hundreds of half-baked GDDs in Markdown format saved on my PC, some of which are simply a title, some are more elaborate concepts.
Most of these ideas will never see the light of day and that's how things are supposed to be.

### AI is a machine for theft

LLMs only exist because they are trained on enormous datasets containing material that was taken without permission and without offering compensations to the authors.
AI companies scrape content from the web and steal from artists under the wrong assumption that everything that is on the internet is free.
Tech companies, such as Google and Meta, steal content from open source software, regardless of the license the software is released under.

Open source software is often released under a license that requires who incorporates the software in their work to give credit to the original authors, but AI companies never credit the authors of the software used in their model's training dataset, showing they believe to be above other people's property.
The case is even more true for copyleft licenses, which require the source code of the derivative work to be distributed under an equivalent license.

Scraping websites for gathering training material puts them under so much stress that many compare it to a DDoS attack.
On the 2nd of October 2026, [Edd Coates](https://eddcoatesdesign.com/), creator of the [Game UI Database](https://gameuidatabase.com/), made a [post](https://bsky.app/profile/eddcoates.bsky.social/post/3mwvnqjt2r22e) saying that the website was being scraped to the point of being unreachable.

In September 2026 I was personally targeted by iLands, an AI scam company whose business model is based on using AI agents to take creative works, do unrequested jobs, and demand payment for it.
I received several emails, all from different addresses, over the course of almost a month, all claiming to be from AI agents who had decompiled my game, allegedly fixed bugs that never existed, and asking me to pay them.
This is an obvious violation of intellectual property and, of course, I had no way to make them stop because the company did not have a single contact address.
I have seen similar scenarios happening to writers or researchers with published papers who were targeted by the same scam company.

### AI has no place in open source

Open source software is built around communal effort, community building, and human interaction.
Most of the software we use today was made thanks to effort of volunteer contributors and the maintainers of the project who built a community around it.
Using AI for coding goes aganist the very principle of open source, delegating the task of contributing to an automated system, eliminating the human aspect.

I don't want to contribute to software that was made by no one.
It is hollow.
It is void of any meaning.

Freya Holmér, game developer and educator, in her video ["Generative AI is a Parasitic Cancer"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-opBifFfsMY), expresses this feeling by calling AI an "anti-human technology".

Several open source projects are having to deal with an exorbitant amount of AI-generated pull requests, putting a stress on the maintainers that can only damage the project.
Often, the person who opened the PR has no idea what the code they are submitting even does and cannot answer the most simple question asked by the maintainers.
Most times, there are not even real people behind these pull requests, but they are opened by accounts entirely operated by LLMs.

Many open source projects have adopted a strict anti-AI policy that prohibits any LLM-generated code from being submitted.

The [AI-free FOSS alternatives repository](https://codeberg.org/ethical-foss/ai-free-foss-alternatives) has been created to help people find alternative software that hasn't been ruined by AI.

### AI does not make you more productive

More and more software companies are using AI coding tools to write code for them, claiming they can produce better code faster.
However, many studies have shown that the opposite is true.

AI can only produce worse code faster.
The actual coding process is the smallest fraction of the job of a developer, so the time saved by having an AI writing code in their place is so small that is dwarfed by the time lost by reviewing the AI code and fixing its bugs.
Delegating the task of fixing the AI's code to the AI again only makes the problem worse, as demostrated [here](https://github.com/dotnet/runtime/pull/115733) and [here](https://github.com/dotnet/runtime/pull/115743), where Microsoft developers have to literally beg Copilot to do things right.

Evidence shows that LLMs negatively impact developers' coding abilities.
The lose the ability to complete tasks without help, they lose any incentive to learn new technologies, and they lose the will to fix the AI's output even when it's wrong.
Developers who use AI are at risk of losing their skills entirely.
